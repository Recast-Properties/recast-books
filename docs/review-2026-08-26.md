### Zero 1099s ever filed forfeits §530 relief and creates payor-level backup withholding liability
**Lens:** Former IRS revenue agent · **Verdict after self-attack:** stands

**Reasoning:** I attacked this hard and it held. The plan does address 1099s — §6 builds a vendor master with TIN and W-9 fields, correctly states that payments Dennis made on the LLC's behalf still count toward the LLC's obligation, and runs a $600 threshold monitor through the existing 3am digest; §10 puts a 1099 worksheet with W-9 status in the accountant's packet; §13 routes it to a filing service. Every open year stays uncured, and §530(a)(1)(B) reporting consistency is tested per year — so every uncured year is a year where the safe harbor is simply unavailable against a Julio reclassification, no matter how good the reasonable-basis argument is. **Fix:** Three changes, in this order. This is the single highest-value item in the whole plan and it is currently absent. (2) Move the W-9 trigger from $600 cumulative to first dollar: the threshold monitor is a lagging indicator and by the time it fires the payment has already gone out untaxed. Concretely, add to the sheet writer a hard block — a GL row with is_1099 true whose vendor record has no TIN cannot be appended without either 24% withheld or a Paul override recorded with a reason and a date. Not a flag in the 3am email; the email is read after the money is gone. (3) Have the accountant price the Julio question separately from the 1099 penalties — 27 payments averaging $218 for directed labor with no invoices is a worker-classification fact pattern, and it should not be buried inside a vendor-cleanup phase where its cost is invisible.
